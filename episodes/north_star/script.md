# The North Star: how Bengaluru ended up building GTA 6

True story, second person in Act 1. About 262 spoken words, so about 105 s at 150 words a minute (the section timings below are rough).
Brackets are visual direction and are not spoken. Every fact is numbered and sourced in the table below.

## Hook · 0:00–0:10

At midnight on November nineteenth, the most awaited video game in the world comes out. [1]

And reportedly, more of the people who built it work in one Indian city than anywhere else. [2]

[Bengaluru at night, a tech park, one window still lit. Crane up past it to a single bright star.]

## Act 1: the star · 0:10–0:35

Imagine it's 1997. You live in Bangalore, and you want to make video games. [3]

Problem: almost nobody in India is playing them. [4]

You start a game studio anyway. India's first. [3]

You call it Dhruva. In Sanskrit, the North Star. "The star that shows the way." [3]

Nobody here is buying games. So you make art for the games the rest of the world plays. [5]

## Act 2: Rockstar comes to India · 0:35–1:05

Across town, Indian artists at Technicolor start building worlds for Rockstar Games. Red Dead Redemption. L.A. Noire. Max Payne 3. [6]

In 2012, Rockstar gives them a team of their own. About eighty people, working only for Rockstar. [6]

In 2016, Rockstar stops renting. It opens its own studio in Bangalore: Rockstar India. [7]

Two years later, Red Dead Redemption 2 comes out. Scroll the credits, and you'll find the names. [8]

## Turn: the star changes hands · 1:05–1:25

And Dhruva? It's been sold to a Swedish company, and by 2019, that company is fighting to survive. [9]

So it sells India's first game studio to Rockstar. For seven point nine million dollars. [9]

Three hundred people walk in. Rockstar India is now eight hundred strong. [9]

## Act 3: the big one · 1:25–1:55

Then Rockstar starts its biggest game yet. Grand Theft Auto Six. Thirteen years after GTA Five. [1] [10]

Reports say about sixteen hundred of its developers sit in Bengaluru. [2]

That's more than Rockstar's studio in Scotland, where GTA was born. [2] [11]

## Bookend · 1:55–2:05

Dhruva. The star that shows the way. [3]

Turns out, it was pointing at Vice City.

[Back to the opening: the same Bengaluru skyline and the same star. A launch-night living room glows below it.]
