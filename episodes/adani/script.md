# "Kidnapped. Trapped in the Taj. Still Standing." Gautam Adani: script v3 (for approval)

Format: toon Short, 9:16, English narration, true story in the present tense (hook in the past tense).
Pace: brisk voice, about 165 words a minute, a new shot every 1.8 s or less.
Tone: fast and fun for the climb; restrained for the abduction and 26/11; neutral and exact for the cases.
Earlier draft: `script_v2.md`.

What changed from v2: the hook promises three blows and the story is built on them; the climb is about a third shorter;
the 26/11 night has the coffee call-back and the kitchen-to-basement escape; the cases are tighter; the loop reads
as one sentence.

Title options:
1. Kidnapped. Trapped in the Taj. Still Standing.
2. Asia's Richest Man Was Once Kidnapped
3. One Coffee Kept Him Inside the Taj

`[n]` = planned shots for the line, separated by `/`. **K** = a Kimodo motion. **INSERT** = one giant object or number
on a plain textured stage.

## Script

**HOOK**

1. Asia's richest man was once kidnapped at gunpoint.
   *[3: night, a white car brakes in a headlight beam / his face fills the back window / low angle, two silhouettes
   step into the light]*
2. He was trapped inside the Taj on twenty-six eleven.
   *[2: the Taj at night, one window dark / his face behind a door, lit by a thin strip of light]*
3. And after one report, his companies lost over a hundred billion dollars.
   *[2: a thick report slams onto a desk / the tower under him drops a floor]*
4. Gautam Adani is still standing.
   *[2: dust clears around his shoes / crane up past him to a whole port working]*

**THE CLIMB**

5. At sixteen, he leaves for Mumbai to sort diamonds.
   *[3: INSERT a giant `16` / he runs along a platform and leaps onto the train (K) / lamp close-up, a diamond
   flashes in tweezers]*
6. Then his brother buys a plastics factory and calls him home.
   *[2: the phone rings among the diamonds / the brother waves him in beside the machine]*
7. The factory needs raw plastic, so he starts shipping it in.
   *[3: the machine coughs to a stop / he hauls a cargo net on a rope (K) / the machine roars, film buries him]*
8. Soon he's trading everything: metal, cloth, even grain.
   *[3: steel coils thunder past / cloth bales fly over his head / top-down, grain sacks bury the street]*
9. But traded goods need a port.
   *[2: a cart piled sky-high races to the water / it stops dead at the edge, the stack wobbles]*
10. In nineteen ninety-five, he wins a contract to run one, at Mundra.
    *[3: grand empty wide of mudflats and sea / he drives a survey flag into the ground (K) / contact close-up, mud
    splashes]*
11. It starts with one jetty. Today, it's India's largest private port.
    *[4: piles slam down / one small ship noses up to one thin jetty / same frame, cranes rise / night floodlights
    snap on, high wide]*

**BLOW ONE**

12. Then, January nineteen ninety-eight. He drives out of a club in Ahmedabad.
    *[3: INSERT a calendar page, `JAN 1998` / the lit club door / the car rolls into the dark]*
13. Gunmen take him, and an associate.
    *[2: headlights block the road / the car doors open, hard cut to black]*
14. A day later, both are free.
    *[2: INSERT a clock spinning a full day / dawn, two figures on an empty street]*

**BLOW TWO**

15. Ten years on, he's having dinner at the Taj with the boss of Dubai's port company.
    *[3: the Taj at night, grand / warm dining room, two men at a table / he laughs, lifts a cup]*
16. He pays the bill and gets up to leave.
    *[2: INSERT the bill folder snaps shut / his chair scrapes back, feet turn to the exit]*
17. Then associates call him back for one more coffee.
    *[2: a hand waves from the table / he sits; coffee pours, close]*
18. And the attack begins.
    *[2: INSERT the cup trembling, huge / the chandelier sways and the lights cut]*
19. Staff rush him through the kitchen, down to the basement.
    *[3: a torch swings, guests follow / through steel counters and hanging pans / down a narrow stair]*
20. He hides all night, until commandos get him out in the morning.
    *[3: he sits against the wall in the dark / boots on the stair / a door opens to grey morning and a commando's
    hand]*
21. Back home, he says, "I saw death at a distance of just fifteen feet."
    *[1: quiet close on his face, slow push. No gag]*

**THE EMPIRE**

22. And then he goes even bigger. Power plants. A coal mine in Australia. Mumbai's airport. Cement.
    *[5: he plants his feet (K) / cooling towers land / a mine excavator bites / a plane skims the airport tower / a
    cement works slams down]*
23. By twenty twenty-two, he's passed Mukesh Ambani.
    *[2: two caricatures on rising platforms / Adani's shoots past]*
24. Soon only two people on Earth are richer.
    *[2: low angle up the platform into the clouds / INSERT a giant `3`]*

**BLOW THREE**

25. Five months later, that report lands.
    *[2: top-down, the report falls through cloud / it hits his platform, everything shakes]*
26. Hindenburg Research, a US short seller, accuses his group of stock manipulation and fraud.
    *[3: the report cover / pages fan open / his face, shocked]*
27. The group fires back with a four hundred and thirteen page denial.
    *[2: he slaps down a stack taller than the report / INSERT counter to `413`]*
28. The shares crash anyway.
    *[3: a Dalal Street ticker plunges / the platform drops like a lift, he flails (K) / INSERT `$100 BILLION+`]*
29. So he cancels a fully subscribed twenty thousand crore share sale, and returns the money.
    *[3: investors queue at a counter / he hauls the shutter down (K) / bundles slide back across the counter]*
30. In twenty twenty-four, US prosecutors charge him over an alleged two hundred and fifty million dollar bribery
    scheme. His group calls it baseless.
    *[4: a US federal courthouse, low angle / a sealed indictment tears open / INSERT `$250 MILLION`, stamp `ALLEGED`
    / a statement sheet, `BASELESS`. No cash, no handover]*

**THE TURN**

31. But it turns. India's market regulator clears the group on key Hindenburg claims.
    *[3: the SEBI building / an order is stamped / the platform climbs again]*
32. In America, he agrees to pay six million dollars to settle a civil case, without admitting or denying wrongdoing.
    *[4: a US court door / INSERT a cheque, `$6 MILLION` / he signs / close on the line "without admitting or
    denying"]*
33. And a judge dismisses the criminal charges, while criticising how the government dropped them.
    *[3: gavel falls, file closes / the judge frowns / a finger taps a page of the opinion]*

**LOOP**

34. Today, Bloomberg ranks him Asia's richest man again. And it's easy to forget that...
    *[3: high wide of the port at night / crane to the road beside it / a white car's headlights swing in, matching
    frame 1]*

## Choices and simplifications (check these)

- **"Never finished school" is gone.** The Week / PTI (2024) calls him a school dropout; ThePrint / PTI (2023) says he
  dropped out of college.
- **"Asia's richest" depends on the index** (Bloomberg yes, Forbes no). Line 34 names Bloomberg; line 1 does not, to
  stay short, so put "Bloomberg ranking" on screen there. Check both on the upload date.
- Line 1 describes who he is now; in 1998 he was not Asia's richest.
- **"But" and "so" elsewhere follow sourced links** (factory to imports; share fall to the cancelled sale). No motive is
  given for the abduction, the attack, the report or the charges.
- The coffee is told as sequence only: no line says it saved his life.
- Line 21 is his own quote. Line 15 "Dubai's port company" stands for Dubai Port; "ten years on" is 10 years 11 months.
- **No ransom** (reported, payment never established) and **no accused is named or drawn**. The acquittals (2005, 2018),
  line 17 in v2, are cut with it.
- **Left out:** allegations of political favour (the group and the government deny them), the 2012 SFIO case,
  environmental fights over the Australian mine, the OFAC settlement, and charges against other US defendants that
  were still being litigated (appeal, Sept 2026). Critics' biggest theme, political favour, is therefore absent.
