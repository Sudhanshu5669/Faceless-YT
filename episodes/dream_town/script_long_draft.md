# In This Town, Nobody Is Allowed to Dream

Draft script — awaiting revision. Bracketed text describes visuals and is not spoken.

## The rule

In this town, nobody is allowed to dream.

If you do, a yellow dot appears above your head.

And the next morning, the king’s soldiers take you away.

[At dawn, a soldier spots a yellow light above a sleeping man. His family clings to him as he’s led toward a castle looming over the town on a distant hill.]

Nobody ever comes back from that castle.

So every night, people drink bitter tea to keep their dreams away.

I don’t.

Because no matter how much I dream… the yellow dot never appears.

## Maya

And my dreams are different.

I know I’m dreaming. I can fly, build castles, even turn the ocean upside down.

[The narrator steps off a cliff and flies over an upside-down sea. A girl with luminous wings waits on a floating island.]

And every night, I meet my friend Maya.

She’s an angel.

At least, that’s what she told me.

One night, I ask her, “Why don’t I get a yellow dot?”

She smiles and says, “Don’t worry about it.”

Then she teaches me how to make a star fall into my hands.

## The dot

One night, I fall asleep beside my little sister.

I wake up to the sound of rain.

Except… it isn’t raining.

[Water runs *up* the bedroom window. Outside, rain hangs motionless in the air.]

I turn to wake her.

And there it is.

A yellow dot, glowing above her head.

She’s dreaming.

I shake her awake, wrap a blanket around her head, and pull her out into the street.

But the dot shines right through it.

[The yellow light glows through the fabric. At the end of the lane, a soldier turns.]

## The chase

We run.

Through the market, over rooftops, down the stone steps toward the river.

My sister can barely keep up, so I carry her.

Behind us, soldiers pour out of every street.

I see a boat. We’re almost there—

[A spear strikes the rope holding the boat. It drifts away from the dock.]

A soldier grabs my sister.

I hit him. I scream. I try to pull her back.

But there are too many of them.

They take her toward the castle.

And leave me on my knees in the rain.

## The bargain

I have to find Maya.

I close my eyes and do what she taught me: breathe… and fall asleep while I’m still awake.

[Mud pools around the narrator. The sound of the soldiers fades. Suddenly, they’re standing on the floating island.]

She’s waiting for me.

“Maya, they took my sister. Please help me.”

She holds out her hand.

“I can. But you have to let me use your body.”

I don’t even hesitate.

“Yes.”

## Maya’s true face

I open my eyes.

But I can’t move.

My hand rises on its own. I feel Maya smile through my mouth.

Then the street explodes.

[Stone erupts around the narrator. The soldiers are thrown back as Maya lifts into the air, using their body.]

She tears through the town, ripping roofs from houses and hurling towers toward the castle.

People run. I can see them, hear them—

But I can’t stop her.

“Maya! What are you doing?”

Her wings turn black.

And for the first time, I see her real face.

Maya isn’t an angel.

She’s a dream demon from the underworld.

Long ago, she found a way into our world through dreams. But she needed a body to stay.

And she’d been waiting inside mine.

[In the dream world, the yellow light above the narrator’s head flickers into view. Maya closes her hand around it, hiding it.]

She was the reason my yellow dot never appeared.

## The king’s army

The king steps out onto the castle walls.

He knows what Maya is.

Centuries ago, his ancestors’ mages created the yellow dots to find dreamers who could become vessels for demons.

That’s why the king took them.

But as Maya reaches for the castle, its gates open.

And every person who was taken walks out.

They’re alive.

The king didn’t kill the dreamers.

He trained them.

[The rescued baker holds out his hands. A glowing hammer forms between them. Around him, dreamers conjure shields, creatures and weapons into the rain.]

Now he has an army that can pull dreams into the real world.

And every one of them is here to fight.

## The battle

The dreamers charge.

A giant made of stone swings at Maya—

She catches its fist and shatters it.

A woman sends a dragon made of fire across the sky—

Maya tears through it.

They build walls. She breaks them.

They bind her wings. She rips herself free.

One by one, the dreamers fall.

The king draws his sword and steps in front of them.

Maya raises my hand to strike.

Then I hear my sister.

“Please. Come back.”

## The fight for my body

She’s standing at the castle gate.

The king has let her go.

Maya tries to move my hand.

I make it stop.

Inside my own head, I find myself back on the floating island. Maya is there, holding the star she once gave me.

“You can’t beat me,” she says. “This is my dream.”

I look past her, toward my sister’s voice.

“No. It’s mine.”

I grab Maya’s hand.

The island cracks. The ocean above us comes crashing down.

And I pull her out.

[Back at the castle, Maya is torn free from the narrator’s body. She hits the ground in her own enormous, winged form. The narrator falls beside the king.]

## The final battle

Maya rises above the castle, furious.

I can still feel the dream around me.

So I reach out—and pull a sword from the sky.

[The blade forms in the narrator’s hand, trailing starlight.]

Maya dives.

I leap to meet her.

We collide above the castle, and the shockwave blows the rain away.

She’s faster. Stronger.

She breaks my sword and drives me through the castle wall.

Before she can strike again, the dreamers catch her in chains of light.

She starts pulling them in.

So my sister runs to me, grabs my hand, and closes her eyes.

A yellow light appears above her head.

Together, we dream.

[A second sword forms. The dreamers’ chains tighten. The narrator and their sister launch upward together.]

Maya breaks one chain. Then another.

We reach her just as the last one snaps.

And drive the sword through the star in her chest.

For a moment, everything goes quiet.

Maya looks at me—not angry anymore, just surprised.

Then the star breaks apart, and she disappears into the morning light.

## A new morning

The rain finally falls.

The king opens the castle gates, and the dreamers return to their families.

He ends the rule against dreaming.

Instead, the mages teach everyone how to recognize a demon—and the dreamers teach anyone who wants to learn how to fight one.

That night, my sister falls asleep beside me.

A yellow dot appears above her head.

She smiles in her sleep.

This time, I let her dream.

[The camera rises over the sleeping town. Yellow lights glow gently above rooftops, all the way up to the castle on the hill.]

## Estimated duration

Approximately 5–6 minutes, depending on narration pace, pauses and the length of the action sequences. This is an early estimate, before voice timing and shot planning.
