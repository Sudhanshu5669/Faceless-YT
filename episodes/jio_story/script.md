# "How Mukesh Ambani built Jio": script draft v1 (for approval)

Format: toon Short, 9:16, English narration, second person ("Imagine you're…").
Length: ~390 words, about 150–160 s at Short pace. Each numbered line becomes one narration line
(and roughly one or two shots). The *[picture]* notes are the planned sight gags, not narration.

## Script

**ACT 1: the lost dream**

1. Imagine you're Mukesh Ambani. You run refineries, petrol and plastics. But you lost the one business your father dreamed about.
   *[Mukesh (toon) on a mountain of oil barrels, smug → sad. A postcard flutters past.]*
2. In 2002, your father Dhirubhai wanted a phone call to cost less than a postcard.
   *[Framed portrait of Dhirubhai (respectful, no jokes); a 50-paise postcard and a phone on scales.]*
3. Then the family empire splits. Your brother Anil gets the phone company, and you agree to stay out of telecom.
   *[A giant cake labelled RELIANCE is sliced in two; Anil walks off with the phone slice; Mukesh gets a "NO TELECOM" sign.]*
4. So you watch.
   *[Mukesh with binoculars, deadpan. 1-second beat.]*

**ACT 2: the market before Jio**

5. In 2015, most Indians are online on slow 2G. One gigabyte costs about 250 rupees, and every video buffers forever.
   *[Crowd staring at phones with spinning loading rings; counter lands on "₹250 / GB" on "250"; a snail delivers a data packet.]*
6. Airtel, Vodafone and Idea make their money on voice calls. So data stays a luxury.
   *[Three telco towers wearing crowns, sitting on bags of coins labelled MINUTES; a tiny "DATA" box in a glass case with a velvet rope.]*
7. But you see something they don't.
   *[Push in on Mukesh: lazy eyes → scheming.]*

**ACT 3: what he saw**

8. In 2010, your deal with Anil ends. Weeks later, an unknown company called Infotel wins 4G airwaves in all 22 circles of India. Within hours, you buy 95 percent of it.
   *[Map of India: 22 pins pop one by one (counter 1 → 22, lands on "22"); a tiny Infotel shop; a cash cannon fires; "95%" stamp.]*
9. Then your daughter Isha comes home from Yale and says, "Dad, the internet in our house sucks."
   *[Isha (toon) at a laptop: angry; a loading bar stuck at 3%.]*
10. Your son Akash adds: telecom isn't about calls anymore. It's about data.
   *[Akash taps an old phone ("CALLS"); it shrinks; a huge glowing "DATA" box rises.]*
11. So you decide: data should be like oxygen. Cheap enough for everyone.
   *[An oxygen tank labelled DATA; people breathing in through phones; Mukesh: determined.]*

**ACT 4: the bet**

12. You don't fix old towers. You build India's first 4G-only network, where even phone calls travel as data.
   *[Old 2G towers knocked over like dominoes; shiny new 4G towers rise on the word "network".]*
13. You lay three lakh kilometres of fibre. The bill: one and a half lakh crore rupees. You call it the world's biggest startup.
   *[A fibre cable unrolls across India and around the globe (counter 3,00,000 km); a receipt prints for ₹1,50,000 crore; Mukesh: star eyes.]*

**ACT 5: the launch and the takeover**

14. On September 5th, 2016, you launch Jio. And everything is free. Calls. Data. Apps.
   *[Stage launch; three items drop in on "Calls", "Data", "Apps", each with a FREE tag.]*
15. Sixteen million people join in 26 days. A hundred million in 170 days. That's seven new customers every second.
   *[Queue of toons streaming into a Jio store; counter 16 M → 100 M; a clock ticks while 7 toons pop in per tick.]*
16. Your rivals run to the regulator, crying predatory pricing. You say their networks are dropping your calls.
   *[Three telco toons at a judge's desk, crying; Mukesh holding a phone with a dropped-call icon: side-eye.]*
17. And the insane part? You even give away the phone. The JioPhone, at an effective price of zero.
   *[Phone on a pedestal; price tag ₹1,500 crossed out → counter to ₹0; Mukesh: wink.]*
18. Data crashes from 250 rupees a gigabyte to under 20. India becomes the world's biggest consumer of mobile data.
   *[Counter falls ₹250 → ₹18; the snail from line 5 is replaced by a rocket; globe with India lit up.]*
19. Telenor exits. Tata exits. Aircel goes bankrupt. Even your brother's company shuts down. Vodafone and Idea merge just to survive.
   *[Telco towers fall over one per name; Vodafone and Idea towers glue together, sweating.]*
20. By 2019, Jio is number one.
   *[Podium: Jio on top; Mukesh: cool.]*
21. Then Facebook pays 5.7 billion dollars for a slice. Google pays 4.5 billion.
   *[Two money trucks arrive one per name; counters land on 5.7 and 4.5.]*
22. Today, 524 million Indians are on Jio.
   *[Pull back: a sea of toons with glowing phones; counter lands on 524,000,000.]*

**ENDING: the pun and the bookend**

23. Your father wanted a phone call cheaper than a postcard. You made it free.
   *[Back to the postcard on the scales from line 2; the phone side now reads FREE; the postcard floats up.]*
24. That's why you call it Jio. In Hindi, it means "live".
   *[Mukesh salutes the framed portrait, then cut back to the opening shot: loops.]*

## Things I simplified on purpose (tell me if you want them stated differently)

- **Non-compete length/end:** sources disagree (2005 truce vs 2010). The script only says the deal
  ended in 2010, which is when Mukesh actually re-entered (Infotel, June 2010).
- **"250 rupees a GB":** a rounded average for 2014–16 (₹226–269; ₹255 for 1 GB of 4G in July 2016).
- **"Under 20":** the late-2018 average (₹18.5/GB), not today's price (tariffs rose after 2024).
- **"Your brother's company shuts down":** RCom shut its wireless business in 2017 and went into
  insolvency in 2019.
- **"Seven every second"** and **"16 million in 26 days"** are Jio's own figures.
- **Not in the script:** the later tariff hikes, the Jio Prime paywall (2017) and the debate over whether
  the pricing was predatory. It's a Short, and the regulator fight gets one line.
