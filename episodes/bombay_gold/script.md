# "It rained gold on Bombay": script v1 (draft for approval, 2026-11-25)

Format: toon Short, 9:16, English narration, true story told in the present tense. Pitch angle A (gold cold
open) with the dock clock as the spine (`pitch.md`). Tone: grand and gripping, **restrained about the dead**:
no jokes, no gags on victims; the spectacle is the ship, the fire, the blast and the gold; the heart is the
firemen and the man who gave the reward away.

About 380 words, so roughly 2:20 (Mangalyaan ran 2:35 at 81% stayed-to-watch). Trim after the voice take if long.
Numbers in brackets point to the facts table in `research.md`. *[Picture]* notes are the planned shots, not narration.

**Hook rule (from our own analytics, toon-short skill):** line one is the shock, under 2 s, with the payoff
already on screen in the first frame. No date, no place-setting, no news summary first.

## Script

**HOOK**

1. It rained gold on Bombay.
   *[Frame 1 is already the money shot: low angle from a Girgaum street at golden hour, a black smoke column over
   the rooftops, and gold bars tumbling end over end through the smoke, catching the sun. On "gold" the nearest
   bar fills the lens.]*
2. Real gold bars, falling out of the sky. One smashed into this man's home. [21]
   *[Inside a Girgaum flat: a retired Parsi gentleman (white sadra, black cap, spectacles) at his window with the
   newspaper. On "smashed", a bar bursts through the wall in a spray of plaster and skids to his feet. His face:
   shocked → stare.]*
3. What he did next, almost nobody would do. But first: where did the gold come from?
   *[He picks the bar up, both hands, it's heavy (squash on lift). Whip-pan out of the window toward the smoke →
   transition to the dock.]*

**ACT 1: THE SHIP**

4. It's April 14, 1944. World War Two. A British cargo ship, the Fort Stikine, is tied up in Bombay's Victoria Dock. [1, 2]
   *[Grand high wide: Victoria Dock at noon, a forest of cranes and masts, dozens of ships, hundreds of dockworkers,
   haze. Crane down onto the Fort Stikine's hull and the name on her bow.]*
5. In her holds: about fourteen hundred tons of explosives. Torpedoes, mines, shells. [3]
   *[Cutaway: the hull turns translucent like an X-ray; crates of shells, mines and torpedoes stack up as a crane
   lowers them; counter lands on 1,400 TONS.]*
6. Eight thousand seven hundred bales of raw cotton. [4]
   *[Dockworkers on the quay heave cotton bales into a sling; the bales fill the hold; counter lands on 8,700.]*
7. And thirty-one crates of gold. [5]
   *[Armed guards watch a crane lower small, heavy crates; one lid shifts and gold glints; counter lands on 31; ding.]*
8. The cotton is packed underneath the explosives. And she isn't flying the red flag that warns of danger on board. [25]
   *[X-ray again: cotton at the bottom, munitions on top. Tilt up to her mast: an empty halyard where the red flag
   should be, flapping in the wind.]*

**ACT 2: THE FIRE**

9. At twelve-thirty, an officer on the next ship sees smoke coming out of hold number two. The hold with the gold. [7, 5]
   *[The dock clock tower reads 12:30. On the neighbouring ship's bridge, an officer lowers his binoculars. Thin
   smoke curls from the Stikine's hold vents. Push in through the vent to the gold crates in the smoke.]*
10. It's more than an hour before anyone raises the alarm. [7]
    *[Clock 13:45. A dock officer rings the fire bell, hard.]*
11. Firemen race in. Fireboats pull alongside. They pour more than nine hundred tons of water into the ship, and still can't find the fire. [20 (W1: 900 tons of water), W2 (fireboats)]
    *[Brass-bell fire engines tear down the quay; firemen drag hoses up the gangway; fireboats arc water over the deck;
    counter: 900 TONS. Inside the hold: steam and smoke, no flame to aim at.]*
12. Down in the hold, the shells start going off. At three-fifty comes the order: abandon ship. [8; W2 14:45]
    *[Pops of light inside the smoke. Clock 15:50. The officer shouts; firemen and crew run down the gangway.]*
13. Sixteen minutes later. [9]
    *[Total stillness for a beat: the clock's minute hand ticks onto 4:06.]*

**ACT 3: THE BLAST**

14. Four-oh-six p.m. The Fort Stikine explodes. The ship is torn in two. [9, 11]
    *[The high wide again. A white flash, then a fireball and a rolling dome of smoke; the shockwave races across the
    water; the hull splits; camera shake (big hit). Hold on the mushroom of smoke over the city.]*
15. The blast throws another ship, the Jalapadma, out of the water and onto the dock. [11]
    *[The Jalapadma lifts on a surge of water and crashes down onto the quay, crushing a crane.]*
16. Windows shatter twelve kilometres away. In Shimla, seventeen hundred kilometres away, instruments feel the ground shake. [15]
    *[The shockwave rolls over Bombay's rooftops; a shop window bursts (12 KM counter). Cut to Shimla: a seismograph
    needle in a quiet observatory jumps and scrawls a spike (1,700 KM).]*
17. Burning cotton falls across the city. And with it, gold. [12, 21]
    *[Flaming bales arc over rooftops and land on tiled roofs; then the gold bars tumbling through smoke: the hook
    shot again, now with the story behind it.]*
18. Less than half an hour later, a second explosion. [10]
    *[Clock past 4:30. A second fireball from the wreck, the smoke column doubles.]*

**ACT 4: THE COST**

19. Thirteen ships are lost. The docks burn for three days. [14, 20]
    *[Night: the dock a sea of fire, sunken masts silhouetted; a counter ticks to 13.]*
20. More than seven hundred people are killed. Eighty thousand lose their homes. [17, 19]
    *[Dawn: smoke over flattened warehouses and homes; families with bundles walking out of the district. No faces
    in close-up; the counters (700+, 80,000) sit quietly in the sky.]*
21. Sixty-six of the dead are firemen. That's why, every April 14th, India marks National Fire Service Day. [18]
    *[66 brass fire helmets laid in rows on the quay at dawn; slow crane up over them. Hold for the breath.]*

**THE TURN**

22. And the gold bar in Girgaum? Its owner carries it to the police. [21]
    *[The Parsi gentleman walks into a 1940s police station holding the bar in both hands and sets it on the desk.
    The inspector's face: shocked.]*
23. They reward him nine hundred and ninety-nine rupees. He gives every rupee to the relief fund for the victims. [21]
    *[The inspector writes a cheque: Rs 999 (counter). Outside, the man drops it into a RELIEF FUND collection box
    under a banner, and walks away. His face: calm.]*

**CODA (loops to the hook)**

24. And the gold kept turning up. As late as 2011, dredgers in Bombay's docks were still pulling the ship's gold bars out of the mud. [22]
    *[Present-day Mumbai port at dusk: a dredger's grab bucket rises out of the water dripping mud; a gold bar glints
    in it; counter 2011. Tilt up from the bar to the sky, where smoke and falling gold fade in: loop to line 1.]*
