## Description

On 11 May 1998, India set off three underground nuclear tests at Pokhran, in the Thar desert. The shaft for the
thermonuclear device was code-named "White House". America's spy satellites had been watching the site for years,
and still missed it.

The team worked at night, put the heavy equipment back on the same spot by dawn, shaped the dug sand like wind-made
dunes, stopped work whenever a satellite passed overhead, and played cricket so the range looked routine. The
scientists wore army uniforms under fake names. Then the wind blew the wrong way, and everyone waited six hours.
