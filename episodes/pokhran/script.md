# "India blew up the White House": script v2 (approved 2026-11-22; hook revised: no date, the feed shows the real White House, then rewinds to Pokhran)

Format: toon Short, 9:16, English narration. Second person ("you" = India's test team), told as a
heist: proud, fast, lightly cheeky toward the spies, never jokey about the weapon itself. Each
numbered line becomes one narration line (one or two shots). The *[picture]* notes are the planned
sight gags, not narration. About 400 words, roughly 155-165 s (we trim after the voice take if long).

## Script

**HOOK**

1. India blew up the White House. And America only found out when India announced it.
   *[Cold open on a US analyst's satellite feed: America's White House from orbit, the South Lawn and the
   portico. On "blew up" the ground domes and a dust ring rolls out. Reverse: the analyst asleep at his desk; the
   TV behind him flicks on: Vajpayee at microphones, on "announced".]*
2. Not that White House. A shaft in the Rajasthan desert, two hundred metres deep, code-named "White House". With a hydrogen bomb inside.
   *[Back to the feed: the blast rewinds like a VHS tape and sucks back into the roof; the feed glitches, the
   coordinates change, and it is now the Pokhran range. Then the range at noon: a steel shaft plate with WHITE HOUSE
   chalked on it; the camera dives down the shaft; depth counter lands on 200 m.]*

**ACT 1: CAUGHT**

3. Rewind to 1995. Imagine you're India's nuclear team, getting ready to test right here. But American spy satellites are watching.
   *[1995, same desert: engineers at a shaft. High above, a spy satellite glints; its lens turns toward them; a shutter click.]*
4. The New York Times prints it. The US ambassador shows up with the photos. President Clinton calls your Prime Minister. Test cancelled.
   *[A newspaper slaps onto a desk; the ambassador slides a satellite photo across a South Block desk;
   a phone rings in Narasimha Rao's office; out in the desert, engineers shovel the sand back in.]*
5. Now it's 1998. A new Prime Minister, Atal Bihari Vajpayee, gives the order: do it again. Same desert. Same satellites. But this time, they must see nothing.
   *[Vajpayee nods once across a desk to Kalam and Chidambaram. Cut to the desert at dusk: the satellite
   crosses overhead again; the team looks up.]*

**ACT 2: THE HEIST**

6. So you work only at night. And by sunrise, every truck is back in its exact parking spot, so the morning photos look the same.
   *[Night work under red torches. At dawn a truck reverses precisely into its own old tyre marks. Two satellite photos, stacked: identical.]*
7. The sand you dig out? Shaped by hand into dunes, the way the wind would make them. The shafts hide under camouflage nets.
   *[Jawans pat a sand pile into a wind-swept dune like a beach sandcastle; a camouflage net drapes over a shaft on "nets".]*
8. When a satellite passes overhead, work stops. In between, the soldiers play cricket, so the pictures show a bored army camp.
   *[Signature gag: someone shouts "Statue!" and every man freezes mid-shovel as the satellite's glint passes.
   Then a gully cricket match in the sand, stumps from tent poles; from orbit it looks like nothing.]*
9. Your scientists arrive dressed as soldiers. Even India's missile man, Abdul Kalam, gets a fake army name: Prithviraj.
   *[Scientists in baggy fatigues climb out of a jeep; one adjusts a cap that's too big. Push in on Kalam's
   uniform name tape: PRITHVIRAJ, on the word.]*
10. The bombs arrive at dawn: flown in from Mumbai on an air force plane, then driven into the desert by an army convoy.
   *[An IAF AN-32 lands at Jaisalmer at sunrise; crates go into four army trucks; the convoy rolls out in a long line of dust.]*
11. Three go down into the shafts. Code names: White House, Taj Mahal, and Kumbhkaran.
   *[A device lowered in a cage; chalked lid names land on each word. On "Kumbhkaran" (the giant who sleeps
   for months), the jawan guarding that shaft lets out a huge yawn.]*

**ACT 3: THE WIND**

12. May 11th. Everything is set for 9 a.m. Then the wind blows the wrong way. If anything leaks, it must not drift toward people.
   *[Countdown clock hits 09:00 and freezes. A windsock swings and points at a village on the horizon.]*
13. So the most secret team in India just waits. Six hours. Staring at the wind.
   *[Bunker: a row of scientists stare at the windsock through a slit; clock counter runs 09:00 → 15:00; faces bored → hopeful.]*
14. At 3:45 p.m., the wind turns. The key turns. The desert jumps. In Khetolai, the nearest village, people say their walls cracked.
   *[Windsock swings away; a hand turns the key; the ground heaves into a dust dome. In Khetolai, a steel pot
   of water ripples and a crack runs up a mud wall.]*

**ACT 4: THE ANNOUNCEMENT**

15. Hours later, Vajpayee walks out to the cameras: "Today, at 1545 hours, India conducted three underground nuclear tests in the Pokhran range."
   *[The lawn of the Prime Minister's residence, a bank of microphones and flashbulbs; the quote in a lower-third card, verbatim.]*
16. In Washington, the phones start ringing. America's spies had missed the whole thing.
   *[Back to the analyst from line 1: every phone on his floor lights up at once; he looks from the TV to his blank desert feed.]*
17. A US senator calls it a "colossal failure". The CIA's own review finds it simply assumed India would never test.
   *[A Senate hearing room, a nameplate; then a thick report lands on a desk. Analyst: shocked → embarrassed.]*
18. Two days later, you test two more. On May 27th, Vajpayee tells Parliament: "India is now a nuclear weapon state."
   *[Two more dust domes on "two more"; then the Lok Sabha chamber, Vajpayee standing to speak; the quote card.]*

**ENDING: THE PUNCHLINE AND THE BOOKEND**

19. Four years later, the scientist with the fake army name, the one the satellites never noticed, becomes the President of India.
   *[Kalam in fatigues, cap off; cut to Rashtrapati Bhavan and the oath. In Langley, the same analyst watches it on TV and spills his coffee.]*
20. And every May 11th, India celebrates National Technology Day.
   *[A school science fair in an Indian classroom, a kid's cardboard satellite on a string.]*
21. All because of one afternoon in the desert, when India blew up the White House.
   *[Back to the shaft lid: WHITE HOUSE in chalk. The camera rises to the satellite overhead and its feed,
   which becomes the analyst's screen from line 1: the Short loops.]*

## Things I simplified on purpose (tell me if you want them stated differently)

- **"Hydrogen bomb":** India's statement called it a "thermonuclear device". Its yield is disputed (a former
  DRDO scientist, K. Santhanam, said in 2009 it underperformed). The script says what India said it was.
- **"America only found out when India announced it":** US satellites had imaged the site, but analysts
  didn't flag anything; officials learned after the blasts, from India's announcement.
- **"Statue!"** is our picture for "work stopped when satellites passed"; nobody claims anyone shouted it.
- **The shafts:** the deep ones were dug in the early 1980s by another engineer regiment; 58 Engineers
  prepared and hid them in 1998. The script just says "the sand you dig out".
- **Not in the script:** Pakistan's answering tests (28 May 1998), the US sanctions (13 May), and the
  "Buddha has smiled again" message (poorly sourced). Say if you want the sanctions or Pakistan in Act 4.
- **The 1995 call-off:** historians still argue whether US pressure alone stopped it (sanctions and the
  election also mattered). "Test cancelled" states the outcome, not the motive.
