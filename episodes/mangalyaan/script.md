# "How India went to Mars for less than an auto ride": script draft v1 (for approval)

Format: toon Short, 9:16, English narration, second person ("Imagine you're…"), same shape as
`jio_story`. Inspiring, not jokey: the jokes are in the pictures, and the room's tension in Act 3 is
played straight. Each numbered line becomes one narration line (one or two shots). The *[picture]*
notes are the planned sight gags, not narration. About 370 words, roughly 140–150 s.

## Script

**HOOK**

1. A one-kilometre auto ride in Ahmedabad costs about ten rupees. India went to Mars for seven.
   *[An Ahmedabad auto at night; the driver flips the meter, which ticks to ₹10 on "ten". A small gold
   spacecraft overtakes the auto, climbing over the rooftops into the sky; counter ₹7 / km lands on "seven".]*

**ACT 1: the impossible ask**

2. Imagine you're ISRO, in 2012. America, the Soviet Union and Europe have all reached Mars.
   *[Night sky: three flags already planted around a red planet, one per name.]*
3. Japan tried. China tried. Both failed. More than half of all missions to Mars have failed.
   *[Two probes sputter and tumble away on "Japan" and "China"; a long wall of mission patches, more than half with a red X.]*
4. And you've never sent anything farther than the Moon.
   *[A young ISRO engineer (toon) at a window in Bengaluru looks up at the Moon, then past it at a tiny red dot: worried.]*
5. In August 2012, the government says yes. Mars only comes close every 26 months. Miss November 2013, and you wait more than two years. So you have fifteen months.
   *[A wall calendar; pages rip off in a blur, counter 15 → 0 months; Mars and Earth line up on an orrery on the desk.]*
6. Your budget: 450 crore rupees. Less than it cost Hollywood to make the movie Gravity.
   *[Engineer counts a modest stack of notes; beside it a film studio's money pile towers over him; counter lands on ₹450 crore.]*

**ACT 2: the slingshot**

7. There's one more problem. Your rocket, the PSLV, isn't strong enough to throw anything to Mars.
   *[The PSLV on the pad at Sriharikota; it strains, a toy-like arc falls short of Mars.]*
8. So you don't throw it. You swing it. Around the Earth, again and again, every loop bigger than the last.
   *[The engineer whirls a sling over his head; the spacecraft's orbit around Earth grows loop by loop.]*
9. You even delay launch by a week, because one of your tracking ships is stuck in bad weather in the Pacific.
   *[A ship (SCI Nalanda) pitching in a storm; the engineer drums his fingers at the countdown clock.]*
10. On November 5th, 2013, you launch. A month later, the final burn. ISRO calls it the mother of all slingshots.
   *[Night launch, then the last loop: the sling lets go on "slingshots" and the spacecraft flies out towards Mars.]*

**ACT 3: the long silence**

11. Then, for ten months, your main engine sleeps, while you cross more than 600 million kilometres of space.
   *[The spacecraft drifts alone; a distance counter climbs past 600,000,000 km; the engineer's beard grows.]*
12. Two days before you arrive, NASA's MAVEN reaches Mars. It cost 671 million dollars. You cost about seventy-four.
   *[MAVEN glides into orbit on a red carpet; two price tags side by side, stacked vertically: $671 M over $74 M.]*
13. September 22nd. You wake the engine up for four seconds. It fires.
   *[A hand presses a key; a 4-second flame; the room exhales.]*
14. September 24th. The real thing: a 24-minute burn. If it fails, you fly right past Mars, forever.
   *[The spacecraft lines up with the red planet; the flame lights; Mars fills the frame.]*
15. And you can't even watch. Every signal takes twelve minutes to reach you, and part of the burn happens behind Mars.
   *[The spacecraft slides behind the planet; the mission control screen goes to static.]*
16. So the whole room waits.
   *[ISTRAC mission control, Bengaluru: rows of engineers, hands clasped; the PM watching from the back. A clock ticks. 2-second beat.]*
17. Then the signal comes back. You're in orbit. First in Asia. And the first country ever to reach Mars on its first try.
   *[The trace jumps on the screen; the room erupts; the PM hugs ISRO chairman K. Radhakrishnan.]*

**ACT 4: the world reacts**

18. NASA's Curiosity rover tweets: "Namaste, Mars Orbiter!" And you reply: "Howdy, Curiosity? Keep in touch. I'll be around."
   *[On Mars, the Curiosity rover lifts its mast-camera and waves; overhead, the orbiter blinks. Faithful recreations of both tweets stack vertically.]*
19. Four days later, The New York Times prints a cartoon: India as a farmer with a cow, knocking on the door of the "Elite Space Club".
   *[Restaged in our world: two men in a club armchair reading a newspaper; a knock at the door.]*
20. After thousands of complaints, the paper apologises.
   *[A pile of letters buries the newspaper office's door; the apology prints.]*
21. And the insane part? You were built to last six months. You keep working for almost eight years.
   *[Counter 6 months → 8 years; the orbiter circles Mars as the seasons of Earth change below.]*
22. Your picture even ends up on India's new 2,000-rupee note.
   *[A note slides out of an ATM in an Indian market; push in on the orbiter motif on the back.]*

**ENDING: the pun and the bookend**

23. In Hindi, Mars is Mangal. It also means "auspicious".
   *[The engineer from line 4 at the same window, now looking up at Mars: proud.]*
24. Not bad, for seven rupees a kilometre.
   *[Back to the Ahmedabad auto from line 1; the meter clicks over; the gold spacecraft passes overhead: loops.]*

## Fact checks (done 2026-11-18)

- **Line 18 (checked):** the reply's page title reads "Howdy @MarsCuriosity ? Keep in touch. I'll be around."
  ([tweet 514618412417302528](https://twitter.com/MarsOrbiter/status/514618412417302528)); Curiosity's tweet opened
  "Namaste, @MarsOrbiter!" and congratulated @ISRO on reaching Mars orbit ([HuffPost](https://huffingtonpost.com/2014/09/24/india-mars-orbiter-mangalyaan-nasa-curiosity-rover_n_5876672.html)).
  On screen, Curiosity's tweet shows only the verified opening "Namaste, @MarsOrbiter!" plus the
  congratulation, worded as reported.
- **Line 11 (changed):** sources disagree on the distance (602 / 650 / 666 million km), so the line now
  says "more than 600 million kilometres". TMI (1 Dec 2013) to MOI (24 Sep 2014) is about 10 months.
- **Line 19 (checked):** a turbaned man with a cow knocks on the door of the "Elite Space Club"; two men in
  tuxedos inside read a newspaper about India's Mars mission. It ran with the op-ed "India's Budget Mission
  to Mars". Apology: editorial page editor Andrew Rosenthal, on Facebook ([Mediaite](https://www.mediaite.com/online/ny-times-apologizes-for-india-cartoon-after-reader-complaints-of-racism),
  [Washington Times](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2014/oct/6/new-york-times-apologizes-for-indias-mars-mission-/)).

- **Line 20 (checked):** the apology, as shown on screen, is the confirmed sentence from Andrew Rosenthal's
  Facebook note (5 Oct 2014): "We apologize to readers who were offended by the choice of images in this
  cartoon." ([IBTimes](https://ibtimes.com/new-york-times-india-mars-mission-cartoon-comes-under-fire-paper-issues-apology-1699753),
  [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/asian-america/controversial-india-cartoon-forces-new-york-times-apologize-n220296))

## Things I simplified on purpose (tell me if you want them stated differently)

- **"Seven rupees a kilometre"** is Modi's rhetorical comparison, not an accounting figure.
- **"₹450 crore ≈ $74 million"** includes the rocket and ground segment; some sources say ₹454 crore or $71–74 M.
- **"Your main engine sleeps":** small thrusters did course corrections during cruise; the 440 N main
  engine was idle for about 300 days.
- **Not in the script:** the debate about MOM's science output (about 27 papers in its first six years),
  and the viral "women scientists in saris" photo (who is in it is disputed, so we leave it out).
- **The cartoon** is restaged in our toon world, not copied; the paper's real masthead/logo only if you approve downloading it.
- **The ₹2,000 note** is shown stylised (orbiter motif, magenta), not as a faithful banknote reproduction.
