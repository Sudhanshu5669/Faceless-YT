# "One Wrong Line on a Map Drowned 375 Miners": script v2 (rewrite for approval)

Format: toon Short, 9:16, English narration, true story told in the present tense.
Tone: gripping, grand in scale, **restrained about the dead**: no gags on victims; the spectacle is
the hidden underground ocean, the broken barrier, the airlifted pumps and the 23-day race to drain
the shafts; the sting is the bad map and the 37-year wait for a ₹5,000 fine.

About 365 words, roughly 2:25 at ~150 wpm (trim after the voice take if long).
`*[Picture]*` notes are the planned Godot shots, not narration.

**Hook rule (`toon-short` skill):** line one is the shock in under 2 s with the payoff already on
screen in Frame 1 — no date, no place-setting first.

## Script

**HOOK**

1. An underground ocean broke into a coal mine.
   *[Frame 1 is already the money shot: deep in a dark coal gallery lit by headlamp cones and coal
   dust, the wall ahead cracks like lightning and a solid wall of black water roars straight toward
   the lens.]*
2. Three hundred and seventy-five men were trapped inside. Not one came out alive.
   *[Water surges across the rails and up the vertical shaft; counter ticks to `375`; on the surface
   pithead, the hoist cable snaps taut and stops.]*
3. And the whole disaster happened because of one missing line on a map.
   *[Whip-tilt upward out of the earth into the mine surveyor's office: a blueprint unrolls on a wooden
   desk, and a red ink boundary stops short.]*

**ACT 1: THE TRAP**

4. December twenty-seventh, nineteen seventy-five. Chasnala, near Dhanbad. It's the afternoon shift at one of India's deepest coal mines.
   *[Grand high wide of the Chasnala colliery in 1975: twin steel headframes towering over coal yards
   by the Damodar river, steam and coal smoke in the winter air, hundreds of miners with cap lamps
   queuing at the pit cage.]*
5. One hundred and seventy-two metres below ground, a crew is cutting a new tunnel for fresh air.
   *[The pit cage plunges down the shaft — depth counter drops to `172 M`. Inside the first horizon:
   miners in helmets chip and drill into a gleaming black coal face.]*
6. Right above their heads sit old, abandoned tunnels — flooded with half a billion litres of water.
   *[X-ray cutaway of the earth: above the lit 172 m gallery looms a vast cavern of abandoned workings,
   choked solid with dark water; counter climbs to `500,000,000 L`.]*
7. By law, miners have to leave a thick barrier of solid coal between them and that water.
   *[Back in the surveyor's office: brass dividers span a wide gap on the official mine plan between
   the blue-inked old workings and the red-inked new tunnel.]*
8. On paper, the old workings ended safely back here.
   *[Close on the blueprint: a ruler taps the inked boundary line — plenty of solid coal left.]*
9. The map was wrong. Underground, the old flooded tunnel actually ran nearly fifty metres farther.
   *[The paper plan turns translucent over the 3D rock cutaway: a ghostly extension of the drowned old
   tunnel stretches `50 M` past the inked line — right up against the miners' drills.]*

**ACT 2: THE BREACH**

10. At one thirty-five p.m., an underground blast hits the face.
    *[Pithead clock strikes `1:35`. Down at 172 m: a flash rips across the coal face; rock shatters;
    camera shake (big hit).]*
11. The thin coal barrier gives way.
    *[Close on the wall: fractures race across the coal seam, and the face bows inward under massive
    water pressure.]*
12. Half a billion litres roar into the mine — more than thirty million litres every minute.
    *[The wall blows out. A foaming black torrent sweeps coal tubs off their rails as the camera races
    backward down the gallery ahead of the surge; counter slams to `32,000,000 L/MIN`.]*
13. It swallows the first level. Then it pours down into the second, nearly three hundred metres underground.
    *[Cutaway of the horizons: water fills the 172 m level end to end, then cascades down the inner
    shaft like an underground waterfall into the `291 M` second horizon.]*
14. With nowhere left to go, water shoots straight up both main shafts.
    *[Inside the vertical shaft looking down: black water races upward toward the camera, swallowing
    ladderways and steel guides until it boils near the top.]*

**ACT 3: THE SIEGE**

15. At the surface, sirens wail. Thousands of family members crowd the pit gates, waiting for the lift cage to move.
    *[Grand dusk wide of the colliery yard: searchlights cut through coal smoke, a huge crowd presses
    against iron gates, every face turned up toward the silent headframe wheels.]*
16. Down below, there is no air pocket. Just black water filling every tunnel.
    *[Slow push down the shaft: steel hoist cables vanish into still, dark water; a single miner's
    helmet bobs to the surface.]*
17. Local pumps barely dent the water level.
    *[Crews haul portable pumps to the pithead; thin white streams arc into mud. Cut to the shaft
    depth gauge: the water mark barely moves.]*
18. So the Soviet Union and Poland airlift giant high-pressure pumps — and specialists — straight to Dhanbad.
    *[Night tarmac: a heavy cargo plane drops its ramp under floodlights; crews and foreign specialists
    roll massive industrial pump turbines onto flatbed trucks.]*
19. For weeks, those pumps roar day and night, throwing torrents of mine water back into the valley.
    *[Time-lapse montage: banks of huge pipes blast brown water down concrete channels toward the
    Damodar valley as sunrises flash into floodlit nights and calendar pages tear away.]*

**ACT 4: THE RECOVERY**

20. Twenty-three days after the flood, the water finally drops below the first level.
    *[Counter lands on `DAY 23`. The hoist cage creaks downward past wet, mud-caked shaft walls; rescue
    crews in breathing gear step out into the wrecked 172 m gallery.]*
21. It takes until February seventh just to reach the deeper level.
    *[Calendar lands on `FEB 7`. Torch beams sweep across overturned mine carts and twisted steel rails
    buried in silt at 291 m.]*
22. Three hundred and seventy-five men are dead. Not one survived.
    *[Quiet dawn wide of the colliery yard: rows of white-draped stretchers along the ground, families
    standing in silence; counter `375` sits quietly in the sky.]*
23. After weeks underwater, many can only be identified by the numbers stamped on their cap lamps.
    *[Respectful close-up on a wooden table: mud-stained metal miner's cap lamps lined up in neat rows,
    morning light catching the stamped worker numbers.]*

**CODA**

24. Four years later, Bollywood turns the disaster into the classic film Kaala Patthar.
    *[1979 single-screen Indian cinema facade at night: glowing hand-painted poster boards for KAALA
    PATTHAR above a queue of viewers under warm bulbs.]*
25. Meanwhile, the real court case drags on. And on.
    *[Inside a Dhanbad courtroom: dusty stacks of tied legal files tower higher and higher on a clerk's
    desk while a wall calendar flips through decades — 1980, 1995, 2012.]*
26. Thirty-seven years after the flood, two surviving mine officials are finally convicted of negligence.
    *[2012 courtroom: a magistrate's gavel slams down — counter lands on `37 YEARS`.]*
27. Their sentence? One year in jail, and a five-thousand-rupee fine each.
    *[Two ink stamps hit a judgment page: `1 YEAR` and `₹5,000`. Cut to a brass balance scale: 375
    stamped cap lamps weigh down one pan while a single ₹5,000 paper note barely touches the other.]*
28. All because one line on a map ended fifty metres too soon.
    *[Back to the surveyor's desk from line 3: the red pencil line on the blueprint bleeds dark water
    across the paper, dissolving straight back into the bursting coal wall of line 1 — the Short loops.]*

## Notes on choices and simplifications

- **Units kept in litres ("half a billion litres" / "more than thirty million litres every minute"):** Official engineering figures cite `500,000 m³` (~110 million imperial gallons) at up to `7 million imperial gallons` (~32,000 m³) per minute. Converting both to litres (`500,000,000 L` and `~32,000,000 L/min`) keeps one intuitive unit system so viewers immediately grasp the speed of the flood.
- **Trigger phrasing ("an underground blast hits the face"):** Engineering case studies focus on blasting/cutting a ventilation connection into an inaccurately mapped barrier, while press accounts often describe an underground explosion weakening the barrier. "An underground blast hits the face. The thin coal barrier gives way" stays true to the physical event without inventing an unconfirmed spark mechanism.
- **Unions vs. official toll:** Trade unions argued the true death toll was higher due to poor shift records; the script uses the official `375` count from DGMS. Say if you want a line noting the disputed count or the bail-on-appeal detail after the 2012 sentence.
